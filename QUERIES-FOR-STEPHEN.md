## Two decisions taken on Michael's side — please confirm

**(i) The November 26th diary entry.** Cutting the orphaned words also
removed William's position reading for that day. It has been restored, and
the entry now ends:

> …The Captain is very careful when the squalls come, to avoid them and
> recover his course again. 21 o 5' South lat. 32 o 41' West long. Isle of
> Trinidad bearing N.E. a quarter N. 139 miles."

The two stray words "would permit." were left out, since the sentence they
belonged to is lost. The reasoning for restoring rather than cutting: the
other entries end the same way — December 26th reads "today we are 38 o 13'
south lat. 8 o 3' East long." — so a closing position line is William's
normal practice, and it is the most useful part of the entry to a reader
tracking the voyage. If you would rather it went, say so.

**(ii) The Introduction.** The passenger and death figures were stated
twice in consecutive paragraphs. The second paragraph now reads:

> This then, is a true, sad tale of the emigrant ship, the "JAVA", which
> left Plymouth after leaving London. However, the deaths did not stop once
> the ship had arrived but by landfall we know that 26 children had died
> and that more died in ensuing days. In addition four adults had died
> including a Mr. Watson, a first-class passenger who had asked and paid
> for special care because of consumption and had been denied this.

Three sentences restating the 500 passengers, the 30 deaths and the
steerage/children point were removed. Every fact unique to that paragraph
is kept, and "However" now contrasts with the toll given in the paragraph
above it.
